# Balancing test: names kept on held rosters

Article 6(1)(f) GDPR. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-01; see the privacy page,
section "After an account is deleted".

## What is kept

On the roster of an event that has taken place: the entrant's name, HEMA
Ratings ID, club and nationality, linked to the registration. The data is not
anonymised and is never described as such. After an account is deleted the
paid mark is hidden on that row in past rosters.

## Purpose test

A complete historical record of a sporting event: who fenced in which
discipline. Results, rankings and club histories are read against it, and HEMA
Ratings publishes the same facts. A roster with holes is not a record.

## Necessity test

Nothing less identifying serves the purpose. A placeholder ("deleted account")
was considered and rejected: it keeps the count but loses who fenced, which is
the point of the record. Only the fields the public roster already showed are
kept; address, notes, accommodation and payment details are not.

## Balancing

- The data was public on the roster while the event took place, published with
  the entrant's knowledge as part of entering a public competition.
- The same facts are commonly published by HEMA Ratings and by organizers.
- The data is not sensitive and carries no contact detail.
- Expectation: a competitor reasonably expects a competition's entry list to
  remain part of its record.

Weighed against this, a person may have a specific reason to be removed. The
right to object therefore stays open: an objection sent to
privacy@hemasquire.eu is decided case by case, and a compelling reason
outweighs the record.

## Outcome

Legitimate interest applies. To be confirmed in the legal review
(privacy-baseline task 3.2), including whether public rosters are a joint
controllership with the organizer under Article 26.
